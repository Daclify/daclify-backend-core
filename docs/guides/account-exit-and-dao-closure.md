# Native departure, private handover and DAO retirement

The implementation candidate adds native departure and retirement. Hosted account erasure, the account-exit screens and independent-app cleanup are subsequent work packages. Availability must be checked against the deployed ABI and configured services; this guide does not establish deployment or external-provider qualification.

`leave(runtime, dao_id, member_id)` requires the member's own supported signature. It deactivates that immutable member, removes their bounded native sessions/app scopes, advances their credential epoch and removes their eligible credit/stake weight. Existing claims, deposited stake, receipts and frozen ballots retain their identity and amounts. Inactive members can withdraw claims and unstake under the existing native restrictions. They cannot perform ordinary member operations. A funded DAO does not become closed simply because a member leaves.

## Private administrator continuity

Private administrator departure, deactivation, demotion and automatic administrator transitions require a current recorded preparation. `beginhand` snapshots both administrators' credentials, the successor's registered encryption key, current DAO key epoch/history policy and latest committed epoch. Its expiry is at most one hour; an unexpired preparation cannot be replaced. `scanhand` checks at most 25 committed epochs per call against existing successor grants. A from-join restriction remains in force. Missing grants, forbidden early history or a changed snapshot block readiness.

The selected successor signs `accepthand` with the preparation commitment returned by `GET /v1/daos/:id/handover`. The public `privateHandoverCommitment` helper recomputes the deployment-bound commitment from either the canonical row or API descriptor. Acceptance binds the reviewed preparation and expiry. Readiness is consumed once by the administrator transition. `clearhand` removes only expired preparations and is idempotent when absent.

Native presence and signed acceptance establish recorded continuity. They do not prove correct ciphertext, successful recipient decryption or recovery on another device. The trusted client must verify decryption and obtain the separate human recovery confirmation before submitting a transition. Null means no preparation; missing native support, expiry and stale contexts never mean ready. Fully settled final closure is the explicit handover exception.

## Active → closing → closed

An administrator explicitly signs `startclose`. The separate fixed `daolife` record preserves existing DAO/member serialization. Missing lifecycle metadata means active on supported and historical deployments; RPC failures are reported separately.

Closing rejects new enrollment, app sessions, enabled app configurations, module replacement/removal, commitments, proposals, ballots, work, funding execution and deposits. It permits existing work submission/review/cancellation and settlement, votes and completion of existing ballots, admission-witness removal, grant/round cancellation, existing activity reviews, governing-document cancellation/acceptance, financial exit, credential removal, security-key rotation/commitment/grants and safe private handover. App disabling must preserve the registered target/configuration. During closing, authorized independent reviewers can finish existing disabled-app receipts; protected evidence still requires a fresh app key bound to its current revision/security epoch. Disabling an app in an active DAO continues to refuse its actions. Ordinary profile publication is blocked; own profile removal remains permitted. The runtime checks new reserve/admission/governance-lock callbacks as well as signed instructions, and modules guard their own entry points. Closed DAOs permit only the narrow remaining member exit/profile-removal/financial-exit instructions.

| Module   | Existing operations during closing                                   | Final closure proof                                                                                                            |
| -------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Decide   | Existing votes/finalization; document cancellation/acknowledgment    | No open ballots or pending registered document changes                                                                         |
| Works    | Existing submissions, independent reviews, cancellations and payouts | Proposed work explicitly canceled, or every bounded milestone canceled or paid; agreement/history retained                     |
| Payroll  | Existing controls and due payouts                                    | Every bounded schedule entry paid                                                                                              |
| Grants   | Existing reviews, application/round closure                          | Rounds closed/expired; applications terminal or their round closed/expired; awarded work still needs Works/core reconciliation |
| Endorse  | Existing witness removal                                             | Applications admitted or expired; no new admission                                                                             |
| Activity | Existing reviews, decision links and security-key rotation/grants    | No unresolved review requests; receipts, encrypted evidence references and delivery identities retained                        |

`scanclose(runtime, dao_id, limit)` scans at most 25 source rows per call. Bounded Works/Payroll child collections retain their existing limits of 16/12. Scans preserve business/history rows and may need multiple calls across the module's shared runtime scope. Each module stores one fixed progress record per DAO with code hash, closing transition, stage/cursor and completion; no new secondary index is introduced. `clearclose` reclaims it only after final native closure. These records and handover/retirement metadata use the retained/completion RAM classification. Funded physical RAM and configured completion capacity remain required.

The existing deployment/governance authority must register reviewed module account/code-hash/kind tuples using `setlifecode`. Modules cannot self-approve. `closedao` verifies an actual complete proof against every installed and qualified code hash and current retirement generation. It does not treat a successful unknown action as a proof. Unknown, disabled, changed or unqualified installed entries block closure. Qualification supports at most 64 installed entries; the operator registry is bounded to 256 accounts, with authorized `clearlifecode` removal.

Final closure also requires zero available treasury, reserves, claims, deposited stake and active ballots. Account deletion does not transfer or burn remaining assets. Unresolved assets or liabilities keep the DAO closing. Native-controller and platform-governance responsibilities cannot be retired through a tenant's closure action. Existing authorized controller continuity must be preserved through its separate governance process.

Where hosting is configured, `closehost` requires the configured hosting settler's own action authorization and an exact closing generation. Its opaque reconciliation receipt is an operator attestation about external subscriptions/invoices/resources. The native contract cannot verify a provider database. A zero receipt revokes it, hosting entitlement changes invalidate it, and successful closure consumes it. The erasure workflow must issue this acknowledgment only after actual reconciliation; an unavailable provider keeps closure pending. Shared DAO subscriptions do not cancel when an ordinary member leaves.

## Qualification and upgrades

Existing member/DAO layouts and liabilities are preserved. New code hashes require fresh SDK/ABI pins, compatible installed-module configuration and the native execution/service permission links. Drain or separately qualify pending work/ballots/documents that bind an old executable hash; retaining a row does not override its approved execution domain. Private grants and history are preserved, while profile-key erasure follows its separate [key lifecycle](profile-key-lifecycle.md).

The frozen pre-exit runtime is reproducible with `npm run build:account-exit-legacy`, pinned CDT 4.1.1 and its original `-O=s` flags. Its immutable hashes are checked before fixture publication. See the [execution ledger](../evidence/2026-10-12-mini-app-execution-ledger.md) for actual passed/failed/unrun checks, candidate versions, physical RAM and local CPU samples. Native tests use owned disposable fixtures; live deployment, provider cleanup, account deletion and asset disposition need their own qualified execution and authorization.

Hosted-account deletion and its finite status/restore contract are documented in [Account erasure policy](account-erasure-policy.md). Native closure proofs remain independent of hosted cleanup.
